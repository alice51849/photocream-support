How to make iPhone photos look like film

Phone photos are sharp, clean and evenly exposed. Film photos feel different, and it is not one thing: it is grain, glow around highlights, softer contrast, shifted colors and small imperfections, all at once. Once you know the ingredients, you can get the look on an iPhone without a film camera, either while you shoot or afterwards.

The ingredients of a film look

IngredientWhat it looks likeHow much
GrainFine random texture, most visible in mid-tones and shadowsSubtle for portraits, stronger for street and night
HalationA warm, reddish glow around bright edges such as lamps and skyLight; it should be noticed only on a second look
BloomHighlights that softly spread instead of clipping hardLight
Light leaksWarm streaks or flares at the frame edgeOccasional, not on every photo
VignetteSlightly darker corners that pull the eye inwardsGentle
Tone and colorLifted blacks, softer highlights, shifted greens and skin tonesThis is what makes it read as film

Most “fake film” photos overdo one ingredient, usually grain or light leaks. A believable look uses several, each at a low level.

Shooting tips that make the look work

  • Look for real light. Window light, late-afternoon sun and street lamps at dusk give highlights for halation and bloom to work with.
  • Keep highlights from blowing out. Tap on the brightest area and lower the exposure a little; a film look added later can lift shadows, but it cannot rebuild a white sky.
  • Compose with some empty space. Film-style frames often look better with room around the subject, which also gives a vignette somewhere to fall.
  • Shoot people as moments, not poses. Candid frames suit grain and soft contrast; stiff poses make the effect look like a filter.
  • Decide on the look before you shoot when you can. Seeing the final look live changes how you frame and expose.

Common mistakes

  • Too much grain on a small screen. Check the photo at full size on your phone before posting; grain that looks right zoomed in can look like noise in a feed.
  • A light leak on every photo. Use it on one or two frames of a set, not all.
  • Mixing looks inside one set. A carousel reads as film when every frame shares one look; pick one and keep it.
  • Plastic skin. Keep beauty adjustments subtle so texture stays.
